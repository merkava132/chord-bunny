chord set

Practice only listens for the chords you tick (plus the basic nine). Sus pairs share notes — Asus2 = Esus4, Dsus2 = Asus4, Csus2 = Gsus4, Fsus2 = Csus4 — as do slash chords and their parent, so either one counts.

detection
how it listens

Each 186 ms window of audio is decomposed onto harmonic templates for every pitch on the neck (learned from real guitar recordings), folded to pitch classes, and scored against the chord list. On the GuitarSet room-mic benchmark this gets 87% of frames right with the basic chords as candidates (old chroma detector: 71%). Every extra candidate is a chance to be wrong, so practice mode scores only your ticked chords plus the basic nine; listen mode scores all 53.

A 7th or 9th is one string among five or six, so richer chords are scored with a bonus for explaining more of the sound; otherwise the plain triad would always win. Sus chords get a small handicap because a major chord with a weak third sounds like one. Next chords are chosen so the transition is one you'd meet in a song: same key, or same root.

For the strings, a second analysis runs every 6 ms with one template per string of the shape you're holding. A strum is detected from the spectral flux; each string's onset is timed from the steepest rise of its activation, which gives the direction (low strings first = downstrum) when the strings are far enough apart in time.

What it can't do: two strings an octave apart (the two Gs in a G chord, the C3/C4 pair in C) are spectrally nested, and pluck-to-pluck variation swamps the difference, so those strings are shown hollow — inferred from the strum, not measured. Direction is only shown when the timing is clear; a single mic can't do better than about ±8 ms per string.
